# Phase 2, stage 1: the AY — sound before memory

*2026-08-06 — implemented in simulation. The Scorpion story begins with its
voice, not its RAM: an AY-3-8912 needs no 128K, no SDRAM, and no new wires.*

## Why the AY comes first

Three facts line up:

1. **The AY was a 48K peripheral too.** The Melodik interface bolted one
   onto the 128K ports (0xFFFD select/read, 0xBFFD write) of machines with
   no paging at all. Games probe the chip, not the memory map.
2. **The card already carries players.** esxDOS's `.playpt3` dot command
   and the browser's PT2/PT3 plugins are sitting in `/BIN` — the moment the
   hardware has an AY, the machine is a chiptune jukebox.
3. **Zero new wiring.** The beeper pin becomes a first-order sigma-delta
   DAC at 14 MHz carrying the beeper + AY mix; the existing RC into the
   3.5 mm jack (and the piezo's own mechanics) filters the carrier.

## What was built

- **`rtl/ay8912.v`** — from the datasheet, as usual: three 12-bit tone
  generators and the noise LFSR at fM/16, envelope stepping at fM/(16·EP),
  mixer gating straight off the sheet, register masks so readback matches
  the real chip, and R14 (port A) stored and readable — 128K detection
  routines probe it. Clock: 1.75 MHz (CPU/2, Pentagon convention).
- **SW[8] = AY/YM** (the switch reserved for this since phase 1): the
  audible difference is envelope resolution — AY 16 steps, YM 32. One
  5-bit envelope index; AY mode steps by 2 at fM/16, YM by 1 at fM/8 —
  same ramp rate, double the smoothness. One shared 32-entry exponential
  DAC table (halves every four steps); fixed volumes ride its odd rungs,
  which reproduces the AY's published √2-per-level ladder.
- **Mix + DAC**: `audio[9:0]` = AY (0–765) + beeper (512), saturated;
  the board top runs it through an 11-bit sigma-delta accumulator and the
  MSB drives the old beeper pin. The beeper is now ~50% duty at full
  swing instead of 100% — slightly quieter on the piezo, cleaner on the
  line out, and it shares the pin with three new voices.
- **Port decode**: A15 & ~A1 select the chip, A14 splits address/data.
  A0=1 keeps the ULA away; Kempston needs A5=0 and 0x??FD has A5=1.

## Verification (and two instructive false alarms)

19 unit checks (`make aytest`): masks, tone frequency to ±2%, mixer
gating, DAC monotonicity, envelope shapes 0x0D/0x00/0x0E, R13-write
restart, 17-vs-29 distinct levels AY/YM, noise, and a sparse-ce
regression (1-in-8 enable, the machine's cadence). Machine level: bustest
programs a tone through the real port decode and watches the audio bus
move. CPU level: the smoke ROM now programs the AY via `OUT (C),A` and
cputest counts audio transitions — the full path TV80 → decode → chip →
mix.

Both integration tests initially reported *silence*, and both times the
AY was innocent:

1. The bustest mask check left 0x0F in R1, making the "period 20" tone
   actually period 3860 — the chip was faithfully playing 34 Hz below the
   test's window. Clear registers between test groups.
2. The BASIC-typed end-to-end attempt (`o 65533,7` …) quietly executed
   **POKE**, not OUT — on the 48K keyboard, `O` in K mode is POKE; OUT
   lives in extended mode behind a symbol-shift chord the PS/2 typist
   cannot type. Six commands, six `0 OK`s, zero port writes. The
   CPU-level proof moved into the smoke ROM instead, where the opcodes
   are hand-assembled and beyond suspicion.

## Hardware bring-up

`git pull`, recompile, program. Then, with the card in:

- `.playpt3 /path/to/tune.pt3` from BASIC, or ENTER on a `.pt3` in the
  browser (the PT2/PT3 plugins from the No_MMC set drive the AY ports).
  PT3 files: zxart.ee hosts thousands (Project AY archive likewise).
- SW[8] flips AY/YM envelope resolution live — audible on tunes that use
  envelope bass ("buzz" bass lines).
